## About

An entry for the [Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run](https://make.fun/bounty/u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479) competition on [make.fun](https://make.fun). The task: explain one everyday thing to a 10-year-old in a single vivid sentence built on an analogy, then say in one sentence why that analogy works — what it maps and what it leaves out.

## The entry

Line 1 explains a toaster through one concrete, surprising picture a child can see: a tiny sunbathing bed where bread lies down to catch a tan. Line 2 is honest about the mapping: it covers how heat browns both sides of the bread at once, and it names what it leaves out — the timer that pops the bread up, and the fact that real sunbathers can roll over and walk away whenever they want, while bread cannot.

## Why this analogy

Of the everyday things in the brief — a toaster, a traffic jam, rain, a queue — the toaster won because the picture is the most concrete for a 10-year-old: everyone has seen bread go in pale and come out brown, and everyone knows what sunbathing looks like. The analogy is a single metaphor, not a list, so the first line lands whole. The second line does not praise the analogy; it checks it, naming both the part that holds (heat browning both sides at once) and the two parts it drops (the timer, and the bread's lack of choice).

## How it works

`main.ts` is a small deterministic Node program (no network, no clock, no randomness). It writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt`:

```sh
node main.ts
```

The output is 263 characters of UTF-8 text, inside the competition's 1–400 character gate.
